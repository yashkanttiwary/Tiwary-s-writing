I need to correct something I was thinking earlier.

When I look at the “Controller” and the “Now,” it’s easy to make it sound like they are two different places. Like we leave the Now to go into the Past.

But I realised… nothing can exist outside of Now.

The “Past” isn’t a foreign country. It is a memory arising here, in this moment. The future is a projection appearing here, in this moment. The “Controller” is a thought happening here.

So the flaw isn’t that we leave the Now. 

The flaw is that we only see a fragment of it.

### The Lens of What We "Have"

We are standing in the middle of an infinite reality (Prakriti), but we are looking at it through a pinhole. 

That pinhole is our Memory. 

It is the lens of “what we have” — our stored data, our labels, our conditioning.

I drew a cycle: 

> **Now ➔ Thought ➔ Labelling ➔ Past (Saved) ➔ Observed**

This whole cycle is happening in the Now. But the mind focuses exclusively on the “Saved” file.

* The event happens (in the Now).
* The mind quickly labels it (in the Now).

And suddenly, we aren’t seeing the world as it is. We are seeing a projection of our own memories superimposed over the world. We are trying to fit the explosion of the present moment into the tiny box of what we already know.

### The Controller is the Fragment

This is why the “Controller” feels so desperate.

The Controller is just a fragment trying to manage the Whole. 

It increases its hold, it builds walls, it tries to protect itself… specifically when it cannot process the reality of the moment.

Because the Controller is limited knowledge. And limited knowledge cannot grasp the unlimited Now. So it panics. It tries to freeze the flow into something it can handle — a label, a judgment, a “me.”

### The Registration is Always Late

This explains the delay I noticed.

Registration has not happened because registration happens *after* the event happened.

Physically, the contact happens. The reality is instantaneous. But the “me” who says “I felt that” — that entity is always a split second late. 

By the time I say “I am angry” or “I am happy,” I am describing a ripple that has already moved. 

We are living in the echo, thinking it is the voice.

### The “I” and the Dissolution

This is why those moments of total absorption feel so profound.

When I am deeply watching something, or doing something I love… the lens shatters. 

The “fragment” view expands back into the Whole. 

There is no “I”. There is just the seeing. Just the doing.

In that state, we are not looking through memory. We are just being.

But the body remembers that feeling of dissolution. And because the “I” (the fragment) comes back, it starts to chase that feeling:

* Through sex.
* Through drugs.
* Through scrolling reels.
* Through work.

We try to use external forms to force the fragment to dissolve again. But we are chasing a product of memory. We are using the “Past” to try to find the “Now.”

### The Ripple in the Water

So, what about the “old responses”? The anger, the fear, the desire to control? 

I used to think I had to stop them. But now I see: Even the old response is happening in the Now.

The old response — that tightness, that urge to control — is just a ripple on the surface of the Now. 

It poses as a monster. It poses as “The Controller.” 

But it’s just a movement of energy. It is a memory rippling through the present moment.

If I try to fight the ripple, I am just adding another ripple. I am acting from the fragment again. 

But if I see the ripple as a ripple… if I see that it is just a fragment of memory playing out in the vastness of Now… then the New Response ends.

> *The Controller isn’t killed; it is seen.*
> *And in the seeing, the fragment loses its power to obscure the Whole.*
